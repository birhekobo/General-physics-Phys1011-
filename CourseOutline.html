About the course

Course description and rationale

This algebra-based course provides science students with the basic concepts of physics needed to understand, describe, and explain natural phenomena. Emphasis is placed on measurement, mechanical and thermal interactions, fluid mechanics, electromagnetism, oscillations, waves, and applications of physics across scientific fields.

The course refreshes and develops foundational physics ideas, connects concepts across branches of physics, and builds the problem-solving skills needed for advanced courses and practical applications.

What you will learn

Course objectives

Upon completion of this course, students should be able to:

  • Refresh and summarize preparatory physics concepts before studying advanced physics courses.
  • Explain the kinematics and dynamics of particles in one and two dimensions.
  • Explain basic concepts of electric charge, fields, and potential.
  • Describe the use and operation of cells (batteries), resistors, generators, motors, and transformers.
  • Explain the first law of thermodynamics for a closed system and apply it to problems.
  • Discuss systems that oscillate with simple harmonic motion.
  • Explain applications of physics in science and technology.

Course outcomes

Learning outcomes

By the end of the course, students will be able to:

  • Apply basic measurement techniques and account for uncertainty.
  • Understand fundamental physics concepts and the relationships expressed by physical laws.
  • Describe and explain natural phenomena using fundamental concepts and laws.
  • Apply physics concepts and laws to practical situations.
  • Use algebraic skills to solve theoretical and practical problems.
  • Recognize the relevance of physics across a wide range of disciplines.

How learning happens

Instructional methods and strategies

Teacher activities

  • Use interactive lectures, discussion, and demonstrations.
  • Guide students in project work.
  • Encourage students to express and defend their ideas.
  • Support independent study and active knowledge-building.

Student activities

  • Participate actively through questions, answers, and reflection.
  • Report on and solve problems related to course topics.
  • Learn individually and with peers through practice.
  • Complete practical work and submit a report for each practical.
